# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Grindstone serves a private cohort of 5-7 friends preparing for interviews over an intense four-week sprint. They range from first-time coders to experienced candidates targeting service-IT drives, corporate roles, product companies, and big-tech loops. Most practice happens after work or college, often on a phone for aptitude work and on a laptop for coding.

## Product Purpose

Turn an interview goal into a demanding but achievable daily training plan. Grindstone diagnoses the learner, selects an honest sprint target, schedules deliberate practice, measures readiness from observed performance, and keeps the small group accountable through daily check-ins, Friday gates, and visible progress. Success means each learner always knows what to do next, why it is scheduled, and what evidence is still missing before the interview.

## Brand Personality

Exacting, grounded, and encouraging. The product should feel like a serious training partner: direct about gaps without being punitive, calm under pressure, and quietly proud when the learner proves a skill.

## Anti-references

Do not resemble a generic admin dashboard, a celebratory children’s learning app, a purple-gradient AI product, a passive video-course catalog, or a gamified streak counter that mistakes activity for readiness. Avoid marketing-page composition, oversized metrics, decorative cards, and vague motivational copy.

## Design Principles

- Put today’s next action before analytics, navigation, or explanation.
- Measure, never claim: every readiness signal must trace back to attempts, timing, calibration, or a gate.
- Make productive friction legible: prediction, recall, and teach-back should feel intentional rather than obstructive.
- Keep recovery psychologically cheap: missed work recalibrates the plan instead of becoming debt.
- Change the room with the mode: learning may scaffold, mocks must remove the safety net.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA with keyboard-complete interaction, visible focus, sufficient color contrast, non-color status cues, reduced-motion support, and touch targets suitable for phone practice. Dense interview interfaces must remain readable at 200% zoom and on narrow screens.
